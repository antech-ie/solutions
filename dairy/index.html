Measure what matters

The value sits in the decision, not the measurement itself.

A measurement creates value when it helps a team release product sooner, tighten a target, reduce manual work, correct a deviation earlier or operate closer to an economic optimum.

↓

Quality / release

Shorter analytical turnaround can reduce waiting, retesting and uncertainty around routine QC and product release.

↗

Process control

More frequent information can show drift earlier, giving production teams time to correct the process before off-spec product accumulates.

⚡

Yield / giveaway

When composition or moisture is known sooner, teams can operate closer to target instead of carrying avoidable safety margins.

◎

Resource use

Better control can reduce unnecessary drying, repeated sampling, manual preparation and avoidable rework.

Lab to process

Start with the operational question, then decide how close measurement needs to be.

Laboratory, at-line and in-process measurement are different ways of supporting a decision. The right choice depends on how quickly the result becomes stale and what the delay costs.

STEP 01

Decision

Define the operating or quality decision that depends on the measurement.

STEP 02

People & action

Identify who uses the result and what action the information can support.

STEP 03

Timing

Determine how quickly the result must be available before it loses operational value.

STEP 04

Measurement

Select the simplest measurement approach that meets the decision need.

Dairy Optimisation Review

Review one process decision where better information may have operational value.

A 30-minute working discussion focused on the process, the current measurement route, the people using the result and the potential value of receiving it sooner.

1
Define the decision. What action depends on the result, and who uses the information?
2
Understand the current route. How is the sample taken, measured and communicated today, and how long does that take?
3
Estimate the consequence. What does delay or variability mean in terms of hold time, labour, yield, energy, rework or risk?
4
Identify the appropriate measurement approach. Only then determine whether MOVE-T, MPA III Dairy, BEAM or MATRIX-F II is relevant.
