The ReSolve device in use

Reuse decisions after detection, recorded as they happen.

ReSolve intelligently returns suitable prepared mobile phase between peaks for reuse in eligible isocratic HPLC methods—helping laboratories reduce fresh solvent use and disposal volumes. Its audit trail can support GMP-controlled operation and data integrity, subject to site qualification and approved procedures.

How ReSolve works

One flow. Two measured destinations.

  1. The detector monitors the chromatographic signal during an eligible isocratic run.
  2. Detected peak fractions are directed to waste.
  3. Suitable prepared mobile phase between peaks returns to the reservoir for reuse.

Method suitability and the routing decision must be assessed for the proposed workflow.

QC · method-specific acceptance

Define the analytical checks before evaluation

The QC Manager sets predefined system-suitability and method-relevant acceptance criteria for each proposed HPLC method. QC: How do we know recovered mobile phase is suitable?

Compare fresh and reused mobile phase under a predefined, method-specific protocol. Include system suitability, retention, area, resolution, baseline and any other relevant acceptance criteria. The PMBrc / SETU result is evidence for its evaluated method, not a substitute for your own assessment.

QC: Will this free analyst capacity?

QC: Does the 94.5% figure apply to our methods?

Validation & Change Control

From evidence to controlled routine use

Scientific research informs a method suitability assessment; it does not replace site validation. Define intended use and risk, agree acceptance criteria and validation impact, conduct a controlled evaluation, obtain change-control approval, then qualify, train and monitor for routine use.

  1. Assess the isocratic method, system, risks and defined response when conditions fall outside limits.
  2. Document QC comparison criteria, equipment and computerized-system qualification, SOP changes, training and approval owners.
  3. Retain recovery, reuse and rejection records, audit trail, deviations and CAPA where applicable.
  4. Monitor performance and routing, review records periodically and maintain the validated state.

What to decide

The resource question

Many site reports emphasise energy and water. ReSolve offers a distinct, method-level solvent and hazardous-waste story: establish a baseline, measure the change in actual litres and document it by method, instrument and period. Transport CO₂e can then be estimated where deliveries or collections demonstrably change.

How to estimate CO₂e with site evidence
CO₂e methodology

Can we calculate CO₂e per litre avoided?

Yes, for a defined solvent mix and reporting boundary. There is no defensible universal factor for one litre of HPLC mobile phase: methanol, acetonitrile, water and other components have different production footprints, and disposal routes differ. The brochure quotes 0.5–2.0 tonnes CO₂e per HPLC system per year but does not provide the solvent mix, avoided litres, emission factors or transport assumptions needed to verify or apply that range.

Estimated CO₂e avoided = measured fresh solvent components avoided × their supplier-specific factors + measured waste treatment avoided × its factor + evidenced transport change − any added ReSolve energy and consumables

Start with the litres and composition actually avoided. Ask suppliers for product carbon footprints and waste contractors for treatment data. Model transport from changes in deliveries or collections, route distances and an appropriate vehicle factor; lower litres do not automatically remove a journey. Keep the boundary and factors in the report. GHG Protocol Scope 3 calculation guidance.

Sustainability questions, answered

Separate measured solvent and waste reductions from modelled emissions.

Which environmental results can we measure directly?

Record fresh mobile-phase litres, volumes routed for reuse, and hazardous-waste litres against a defined baseline, method and reporting period. Use the actual change at the site for reporting.

Is every litre routed for reuse a litre of waste avoided?

Not automatically. Recovered flow is a device routing measure; compare actual fresh-phase preparation and waste disposal before and after use to establish the site outcome.

Can we claim a CO₂e saving per litre?

Only for a defined solvent composition and reporting boundary with documented production, disposal and any additional device factors. There is no universal per-litre value. See the calculation approach.

Do fewer solvent litres mean fewer transport emissions?

Only if deliveries or waste collections actually change. Document the journeys, distances, vehicle factors and time period; report this estimate separately from measured litres.

Calculator assumptions, calculation method and sources
How the calculator works

Metrics, assumptions and sources

Use this calculator for a suitable isocratic workflow. All inputs start at zero so you can enter your own volumes and costs. Test the estimated outputs in a site evaluation; they are not measured ReSolve performance or an Antech quotation.

Volumes and direct cost

  • Potentially reused litres per month = monthly prepared mobile-phase usage × entered reusable proportion.
  • Annual direct cost opportunity = potentially reused litres × 12 × (entered fresh mobile-phase cost per litre + entered waste-disposal cost per litre).
  • This calculation assumes each reused litre avoids one litre of fresh preparation and one litre of waste. Measure these two reductions separately during a pilot; recovered volume alone does not prove either saving.

Analyst capacity

  • Potential hours released per year = entered monthly preparation and handling hours × entered time-reduction percentage × 12.
  • Hours are shown as capacity. They are not valued in euros or added to the cashflow; count a payroll saving only when an actual expense is reduced.

Five-year cashflow

  • Net annual cash benefit = annual direct cost opportunity − entered annual service and operating costs.
  • Cumulative cashflow at year N = − entered upfront equipment and implementation cost + N × net annual cash benefit.
  • Payback = upfront cost ÷ positive net annual cash benefit. The chart assumes equal full-year benefits and linearly interpolates payback.

Sources and boundaries

The optional 94.5% study reference beside the reusable-proportion slider comes from the Pharmaceutical & Molecular Biotechnology Research Centre (PMBrc) at South East Technological University (SETU) evaluation: two matched 96-injection sequences in one method. It is not a default value or a rate to apply across other methods. The separate 98-injection ReSolve export records routed volume, not site savings. All usage, prices, labour time, investment and operating costs come from the visitor; no external cost benchmark is built into the calculator.

The projection holds usage, prices, recovery and costs constant for five years. It excludes financing, discounting, tax, inflation, downtime, changing utilisation and any additional device energy or consumables not entered as operating costs. It does not calculate CO₂e, incident reductions or the value of redeployed analyst time. See the separate CO₂e methodology.
