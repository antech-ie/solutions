01Can recycled mobile phase maintain analytical performance?
Analytical integrity · PMBrc / SETU white paper

What changes in the workflow

These are the results of a PMBrc / SETU study evaluating ReSolve™, a system that recycles mobile phase during isocratic HPLC analysis. ReSolve™ diverts the detected analyte fractions to waste and returns the remaining mobile phase to the reservoir for reuse.

How the comparison was tested

The researchers ran a 96-injection sequence using virgin mobile phase, then repeated the same paracetamol and ibuprofen method using mobile phase recovered from that first sequence. The purpose was to establish whether recycled mobile phase maintained analytical performance.

What to look for in the results

Sequence 1 is the virgin-mobile-phase reference; Sequence 2 uses recycled mobile phase. The tables and interactive graph compare retention time, peak area and peak shape, while the volume figures show how much mobile phase was recycled or sent to waste. Each sequence contains 80 sample injections, 13 standards and 3 blanks; the interactive graph compares the 80 samples from each sequence.

Read the PMBrc / SETU white paper (PDF) ↗

The audit extract and 98-injection operational record further down are separate supporting evidence, not part of this SETU comparison.

Independent evaluation by PMBrc / SETU, commissioned by Antech.

✓ Technical conclusion: analytical performance maintained

The study reported equivalent chromatographic performance between virgin and recycled mobile phase. Retention time, peak area, tailing, symmetry and resolution showed minimal differences, with no observed difference in baseline noise or stability. Mobile-phase recycling was 94.5% in both sequences.

Findings apply to the evaluated paracetamol and ibuprofen method.

03What this could change in your daily workload

The analytical comparison addresses performance. The operational opportunity is less preparation, replenishment and handling; the effect on your workload should be measured in your laboratory.

Less preparation variability

Fewer fresh mobile-phase preparations can reduce opportunities for weighing, mixing and pH-adjustment errors.

More analyst time for testing

Less routine preparation, replenishment and handling can release analyst time for testing and investigations.

Longer uninterrupted sequences

Recycling can reduce replenishment interventions and support extended analytical runs.

Less hazardous waste and handling

Lower fresh mobile-phase demand and waste generation can reduce bottle changes, transfers and waste handling.

02.

Circularity starts at the point of use.

Use less fresh material. Keep suitable mobile phase in use for longer.

ReSolve™ recycles prepared mobile phase within the isocratic HPLC workflow. The circular opportunity is to reduce fresh input and prevent hazardous waste while maintaining the analytical requirements of the method.

REDUCE

Less fresh input

Prepare and purchase less fresh mobile phase for the same accepted analytical workload.

REUSE

Retain useful material

Return suitable mobile phase to the reservoir, with detected analyte fractions diverted to waste.

MANAGE THE REMAINDER

Account for the full balance

Include rejected fractions, top-ups, cleaning and final reservoir disposal in the result.

Circularity is also a business decision.

Lower material demand can reduce purchasing exposure, ease replenishment pressure and free preparation time. The value spans sustainability, supply chain, procurement and laboratory operations.

The circularity hierarchy and the laboratory boundary

Refuse → Rethink → Reduce → Reuse → Repair → Refurbish → Remanufacture → Repurpose → Recycle → Recover. The hierarchy prioritises retaining value and preventing resource demand before end-of-life recovery.

This is a framework for explaining the opportunity, not a certification of ReSolve™. Point-of-use mobile-phase recycling, off-site solvent distillation and recovery as fuel are different processes. Their energy demand, purity requirements and environmental outcomes must be assessed separately.

Quality requirements remain part of the design: approve methods and operating boundaries through QC and QA. The supplied sector references describe broader pharmaceutical manufacturing; they do not establish performance or GMP approval for this laboratory application.

04. Calculation method

Investment returns: units = labs × included HPLCs per lab. Initial investment = units × (equipment + installation). Annual net operating cash saving = material saving + disposal saving − annual support. Net benefit over N years = N × annual net saving − initial investment. ROI = net benefit ÷ initial investment. NPV = −initial investment + sum of annual net saving ÷ (1 + discount rate)^year. Upfront payment occurs at year 0; operating cash flows occur at each year end. Simple payback assumes evenly accruing monthly savings. Labour capacity value is excluded from cash returns.

Salary conversion assumption: 2,080 paid hours per analyst per year (40 hours/week × 52 weeks). This fixed assumption converts annual salary into a value per preparation person-hour; it is separate from laboratory operating weeks. Employer contributions and benefits are excluded.

People time: annual preparation person-hours = person-hours per lab per operating week × labs × operating weeks/year. Hours released = baseline hours × the separate preparation-time reduction. Labour cost and capacity value = the relevant person-hours ÷ annual paid hours per analyst × annual salary. Three-year values = annual values × 3. Staff capacity value is excluded from material/disposal savings, cumulative cost charts and ROI.

Annual baseline demand: labs × HPLC systems per lab × flow (mL/min) × 60 × hours/day × days/week × weeks/year ÷ 1,000. Monthly demand = annual demand ÷ 12.

Fresh mobile phase avoided: annual baseline demand × assumed net fresh-demand reduction. Waste avoided = baseline waste × the separate net waste-reduction input. Recycled-flow totals and reuse counts are not added to either result.
